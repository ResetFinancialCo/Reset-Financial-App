How I rebuilt after bankruptcy

When I filed, I worried it would follow me forever. What I learned: bankruptcy wasn't the end of my financial story. I found housing after discharge, financed a vehicle after discharge, and rebuilt my credit score from 438 to 684. Those were my results — not a promise of yours. Your profile, timing, and circumstances are different. The lesson isn't a number: it's that you can learn, organize, and rebuild with intention.

My rebuild reflection

What I wish I knew

Rebuilding is not instant. A credit approval isn't automatically a good financial decision. A credit score is not the whole financial picture. Accurate negative information isn't automatically a credit-report error. And rebuilding shouldn't recreate the same pressure you're trying to leave behind. This time, we're rebuilding with intention.

What looks wrong?

Identity theft is different

Not recognizing an account does not automatically establish identity theft — creditor names can differ from store names, and accounts may be transferred or sold. Never falsely claim identity theft to remove legitimate debt. If you genuinely believe it applies, use the federal identity-theft reporting process.
